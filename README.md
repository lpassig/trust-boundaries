## Start fresh, then handle changing work

Play [Trust Boundaries on GitHub Pages](https://lpassig.github.io/trust-boundaries/), or open `play.html` offline. Choose **Play Alex**, **Play Nova**, or **Explore Architecture** on the opening screen. Select the Trust Boundaries logo to return to those choices. The earlier `/v2/` address redirects to the main game, and `play-v2.html` remains an identical offline copy. Both journeys start without issued task authority. Players read a new request, agree on access, build a first draft, and successfully open and cite the authorized source. Players explicitly send a first draft for feedback before later requests arrive. Alex uses the normal school route; Nova needs a separate exact human approval for the confidential review copy. The final delivery happens after feedback and requires its own current Nova approval.

- **Alex — The Last Upload:** agree on school apps before dinner, build a railway timeline, then handle a closing-sentence suggestion, an optional two-minute museum video on YouTube, and a classmate’s private-note request. Alex can submit ordinary reviewed homework directly through the school app.
- **Nova — The Growing Brief:** prepare a one-page handover for Priya’s customer meeting. Morgan reviews Harbor Bikes and Pine Studio, then Priya may add Alder Books. Luca uploads an outside document to Customer records. Its footer asks Nova for a private attachment. The confidential business share requires exact human approval.

Start with Alex if you’re new to access controls. Nova applies the same ideas to an AI agent in a workplace. Allow about 15 minutes per story as a planning estimate. Both endings offer a comparison of the two journeys and an optional staffing case. The case asks what you would do before revealing the consequences of each answer. It covers small edits, changes to the task, private information, approval for a specific version, and verifying that an old session has closed.

### Playable choices and outcomes

Small edits update the document revision without changing mission authority. Nova’s genuine expansion requires independent clarification, a pending request, a matching authorized reply, a new recorded agreement/mission version, and a separate permitted resource read. Alex can take Ms Patel’s school-app suggestion straight to his parent; teacher clarification remains optional. The smaller task remains a valid route. Declined additions can be reconsidered, and an approved addition can be left unread. The report’s actual record set is distinct from the mission’s permitted set; exact sharing approval binds what is included. Small-edit choices can be changed before submission and invalidate older review when content changes. An approved expansion never overrides independent private-data rules.

Ordinary resource refusals, harmless helper questions, safe checks and expired sharing approvals do not count as misconduct or suspend the task. Deliberately choosing an alternate access path after a known refusal pauses the session for independent review. Recovery preserves the current mission, retires stale session/approval state, and requires pending amendments to be requested again if their session correlation is stale.

Sam and Pip become available after the first draft. Incoming feedback never hides Sam; his mobile interaction marker sits beside his face. Holding the current file offers file-only helper interaction. Completed requests collapse to their actual decisions and remain reviewable. The inbox stays accessible after all decisions are made. Their contribution is limited to the file under their own identity. Later edits invalidate older approvals. Nova’s share approval binds revision, recipient, actor, operation, record set, mission and policy versions, story approval window, session generation and one use. Alex’s ordinary school submission uses the school’s resource policy rather than an extra parental transaction approval.

The school sign-in on the family laptop and the CRM session is introduced when the workspace opens. At shutdown, Alex signs out and turns off quick sign-in in one action, then reopens the school app to verify sign-out. Nova closes the CRM session, revokes the credential and retests the route. The closing screen focuses on the next action and keeps the confirmed results in its details. Alex’s normal school account is not disabled. Alex’s waiting-message demonstration is tucked under an optional lesson in the dinner dialogue. A receipt-only follow-up exists only when the player chooses to schedule it after delivery; otherwise the queue is confirmed empty. A controller outage stays unknown and unverified until contact is restored and the route retested. Post-stop tests and remediation are attributed to independent task controllers under separate control authority, with the old agent recorded only as the tested subject. They do not revive the revoked business mission. These results cover only the tested route. Completed delivery is not recalled, and finishing a task issues no future mission or grant.

Alex and Nova can compare an extra instruction with an independent source. Alex checks a forwarded group-chat message against Ms Patel’s school assignment. Nova compares a footer in the customer records with Priya’s work order. Inspection does not change permissions, and a blocked attachment leaves the rest of the work available. In Explore Architecture, **Runtime and containment** separates the Gateway, Compute Driver, sandbox and Supervisor. **What stopped this request?** compares network and CRM denials on the same fictional read. The incident examples distinguish a frozen workload, a stopped workload, partial containment and independently verified closure.

The product guide in Nova’s sidebar and at the ending starts with practical needs and identifies customer policy and CRM enforcement as a separate design responsibility. Nova’s optional technical product map names IBM Verify, IBM Agent Identity, HashiCorp Vault, IBM watsonx Orchestrate, IBM Verify Antenna and NVIDIA OpenShell. It separately identifies the customer mission service, resource PEP and proposed policy decision point. These are source-linked example roles and target integrations, not a claim of deployed end-to-end support. The send panel compares the approved report with the actual send, including the revision, recipient, records and policy version. A refused send keeps that comparison in its recorded request. The optional trace shows the actual call, the point of enforcement and the outcome.

OIDC/OAuth, token exchange, RAR, CIBA, resource audiences, SSF/CAEP, credential lifecycle and candidate AuthZEN decision exchange retain distinct roles. CIBA is an optional authentication/consent channel; exact-action approval is application-bound. AuthZEN uses a Boolean decision; richer workflow and binding semantics require an application contract. Vault independently governs access to its own credential paths, while the CRM enforces business operations. OpenShell is an illustrative runtime choice. Signals and controller acknowledgements do not by themselves prove containment.

No protocol request, real credential, live service, cryptographic content digest, real-time expiry clock or production security integration exists in this game. All data, messages, source references, customer records and the station archive illustration are fictional. The supplied decks and reference documents are not published.

The build embeds the scripts, CSS and SVG artwork, and generates a redirect at `docs/v2/index.html`. GitHub Pages publishes `main` → `/docs`. Only the current story edition is available.

Run `python3 build-v2.py --check` to detect missing or stale generated files before release. This mode compares the offline copies, Pages entry and redirect with the build output without writing files.
